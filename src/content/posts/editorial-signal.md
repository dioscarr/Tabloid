More content is not automatically more useful. A reader often needs a way to decide whether a post is relevant before committing a few minutes to it. That is why AstroPaper starts with a compact description, a date, a reading estimate, and a small set of tags.

## Make the index do real work

The home page highlights a small number of featured posts and leaves the complete archive one clear action away. A tags page explains the available topics instead of treating categories as hidden metadata. Search filters the local, published index and states plainly when it has no matches.

These are small choices, but together they let a reader recover context without an infinite feed.

## Use original examples

This migration uses posts about Tabloid, AstroPaper, and the operations around a branch preview. They exist to demonstrate the content model rather than reproduce a template's editorial work. Future content can replace them without changing the route structure, metadata, or navigation.

## Preserve a stopping point

A publication should be able to say, “you are caught up.” The most respectful interface gives the reader useful routes, not endless prompts to continue.
